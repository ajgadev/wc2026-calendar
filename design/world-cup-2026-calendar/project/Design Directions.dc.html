World Cup 2026 Live Calendar · design pass 1 · June 11, 2026

Three directions for a broadcast-grade match calendar

All three share the working thesis — broadcast scoreboard, not dashboard — and the same information skeleton. They differ on the axes you flagged: how loud the host-country coding is, retro scoreboard vs. modern broadcast, and how dense the rows get. Each thumbnail shows the identical slice of real data (Sunday, June 14 — Germany's opener day) so the comparison is fair.

Assumptions & ground rules

  • Real data. Groups, fixtures, venues and kickoff times are the published post-draw schedule (Mexico–South Africa at the Azteca today, Germany–Curaçao in Houston June 14, etc.). Scores and scorers for matches after kickoff are simulated for the mockups and labeled as such.
  • Demo moment: Sun June 14, 19:38 ET. The one day this opening week where finished, live and upcoming naturally coexist on screen. The prototype gets a "moment" tweak so you can rewind to opening day.
  • LIVE is amber, never red. Red belongs to Canada in the host-coding system; the live state must not collide with it. Amber also reads as "broadcast" (clock graphics) rather than "alert".
  • States differ by shape and weight, not hue alone. Upcoming shows a kickoff time where the score would sit; live adds a filled badge + running minute + pulse; finished shows FT + a solid winner marker (▸) and dims the loser.
  • Host coding is double-encoded. Row tint and a venue chip naming city + country — colorblind-safe and screen-reader friendly.
  • No FIFA marks, no trophy, no stock heroes. Flags and the fixture data are the only imagery. Squad photos arrive as user-supplied assets; the initials fallback is designed first.
  • Dark-first. Light theme ships as a pure token swap (CSS custom properties → Tailwind v4 @theme).

Recommendation: 01 · Lower Third

It hits the brief's thesis most directly. Expanded-width scores are the loudest typographic voice on the page — legible across a room — while everything else stays quiet. The medium-loud host tint (your pick from the questionnaire) keeps the geography readable from the calendar alone without fighting the live state, and it survives WCAG AA: text never sits on saturated color, only on an 8–9% tint. Floodlight is charming but its warmth fights the blue/green/red host triad; Press Box is the most scannable but mutes exactly the signature element you asked to make structural. Lower Third also degrades best to a light theme — it's a pure token swap.

Built and shipped alongside this doc — see below.

Interactive prototype

One working app, all screens: agenda with the three match states and inline detail, calendar month grid (June/July) with 44px chips, team drawer with Germany's squad grid (photo placeholders + initials fallback), standings with qualification zones, computed stats, sticky filter bar, knockout placeholders and rest-day empty states. Live matches tick in real time; standings and scorers are computed from the simulated results, never hand-typed.

Open the prototype →

Groups, fixtures, venues and kickoff times are the real published schedule. Results, scorers and standings after each kickoff are simulated (and labeled in match detail). Resize the window: agenda is the mobile default, calendar the desktop default.

Token sheet + Tweaks bar

tokens.css is a Tailwind v4 @theme block: ground/line/text colors with contrast notes, the three host accents with the row-tint recipe, live/win semantics, type scale, spacing rhythm, radii, and the two-keyframe motion budget. The prototype consumes the same custom properties, so theme and text size are pure attribute swaps (data-theme / data-textsize).
